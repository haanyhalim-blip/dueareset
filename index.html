Due a reset? Quit, cut down, do more or take on a challenge

Most of us run on habits we never chose. We all want to get a little better, and we go about it in different ways. Some people take a habit on head-on. Others come at it sideways – a challenge, a change of scene, or a new routine that crowds the old one out.

Whether you're quitting, cutting down, doing more or starting something new, that's a reset: a deliberate change, a bit bolder than your usual.

How bold is up to you – what feels extreme to one person is an ordinary week for someone else. What matters is that it's on purpose.

Say what you're resetting, then build your own page to get you through it: a timer, tips, a journal, your weight, a plan, words to keep you going, and a question each day about why it's hard. Nobody sees your page but you. The list of common resets shows what people commonly reset and how they go about it, so you know you're not the only one.

People use it as a quit smoking day counter, to cut down on drinking, for Dry January, a digital detox, a no-spend month, walking every day, a misogi challenge – or simply a fresh start.

Not every reset is about giving something up. Sometimes it's a challenge: take on something hard enough, and everything else can feel different afterwards – including the habits you wanted to change.

One idea is the misogi challenge: one big challenge, taken on on purpose, hard enough that you might not manage it – a long walk, a cold swim, a climb, a trip on your own. It has just two rules: make it really hard, and don't die.

The point isn't the challenge itself. It's finding out you can do more than you thought, and bringing that back into the rest of your life. One a year is plenty – even one every few years can reset how you see yourself.

The idea comes from Michael Easter's book The Comfort Crisis (2021).
